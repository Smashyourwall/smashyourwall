Why Am I Confident in Other Areas but Terrified of Approaching Women?

10 min read

You might be confident in almost every other area of your life.

Maybe you're good at your job. Maybe you're comfortable talking to strangers. Maybe you've played sports your whole life, have plenty of friends, or can stand in front of a room and give a presentation without thinking twice.

And yet when it comes to walking up to a woman you're attracted to and saying hello, suddenly everything changes.

Your heart starts racing. You start thinking about what you're going to say. You wonder what she'll think. You tell yourself you'll do it in a minute.

And then she leaves.

If that sounds familiar, there's probably nothing wrong with your confidence.

You're probably just trying to do something you've never actually learned how to do.

Confidence Isn't Universal

One of the biggest mistakes we make is thinking of confidence like it's one giant trait you either have or don't have.

It doesn't really work that way.

You can be confident in one area and completely insecure in another.

Think about basketball.

Someone who has played basketball for ten years might casually walk onto a court and knock down free throws without giving it much thought.

Put that same person on a baseball field and ask them to hit a 90 mph fastball, and they might look completely lost.

That doesn't mean they suddenly became an unconfident person.

They simply don't have the same experience.

Approaching women can work the same way.

The First Time Is Supposed to Feel Terrifying

When I first started approaching women, I had spent years thinking about doing it.

I'd watch videos of guys walking up to women and starting conversations.

They made it look incredibly simple.

Walk over.

Say hello.

Talk.

But when I imagined myself doing it, it felt almost impossible.

The first time I finally forced myself to approach, I was terrified.

I wasn't suddenly confident because I had decided I was going to do it.

I was scared and did it anyway.

That's an important distinction.

Confidence didn't come first.

The experience came first.

Confidence Is Accumulated Data

Every time you do something and survive it, your brain gets another piece of information.

You approach someone.

Maybe she says yes.

Maybe she says no.

Maybe the conversation is awkward.

Maybe it's great.

Maybe you completely screw it up.

And then you realize you're still alive.

You go home.

The world didn't end.

Then you do it again.

And again.

Eventually, your brain has a lot more evidence to work with.

That's what happened to me.

After thousands of approaches, I had accumulated a ridiculous amount of evidence that approaching a woman wasn't actually as catastrophic as my brain had originally convinced me it was.

I wasn't born with some special confidence gene.

I had simply done the thing enough times that it stopped feeling like something completely unknown.

You're Not Going to Think Your Way Into Confidence

This is where I think a lot of guys get stuck.

They keep trying to become confident before they take action.

They watch another video.

Read another article.

Think about what they're going to say.

Try to get themselves into the right mindset.

And then they wait.

But eventually you have to get some real-world experience.

You can't practice a free throw by reading about basketball.

And you can't become comfortable approaching women by thinking about approaching women.

At some point, you have to walk over and say hello.

So What If You're Confident Everywhere Else?

If you're confident in your career, your friendships, your hobbies, your conversations, or other parts of your life but completely freeze when it comes to approaching women, I wouldn't take that as proof that you're secretly an insecure person.

I'd look at it as a skill you've probably spent very little time practicing.

And if you've spent years avoiding it, your brain has had years to build up the fear around it.

That's what happened to me.

I spent years wanting to approach women and backing out.

Then I finally started doing it.

It took a lot of approaches, a lot of rejection, a lot of uncomfortable conversations, and a lot of mistakes.

But eventually, something that once felt almost impossible became something I could actually do.

Not because I magically became a different person.

Because I finally gave myself enough experience to prove that I could.

If that's where you are right now, I wrote the story for you.
